## Whole-outline assessment

Start with the book's intended readers, prerequisites, purpose and requested scope.
Compare parts, chapters, parents, siblings and descendants before proposing title edits.
Check subject coverage, reading order, granularity, terminology and distinctions between
conceptual explanation, standards/research, comparisons and implementation where relevant.
These roles are book-specific; do not impose the same chapter pattern or heading style.
Follow the saved heading rules and book instructions; nominal preferences do not justify
losing actors, objects, conditions, order or distinctions.
Technical keywords must identify material actually explained. Do not turn input construction
into an optimization guarantee or format convergence into a formal standard without evidence.

The outline is navigation evidence, not proof that a title matches its section.
Read the body context in this same prompt, including relevant descendants, before deciding.
Where omitted tables, figures, code or other sources are needed, record needs-evidence and
the required follow-up; do not invent support from the title or introduction alone.
Distinguish selected editable titles from reference-only titles and covered source positions.
Record each selected heading ID, current title, proposed title, keep/revise/needs-evidence,
reason, body evidence and any separate body/table/figure synchronization proposal.
Review coverage is the selected headings, not every heading visible in the outline.
Chapter moves, merges, splits and hierarchy changes are separate structure proposals only;
this view does not expand edit permissions or relax baseline verification.
Verification remains mechanical and keeps meaning_verified: false.
